Solubility of Some Transition Metal Oxides in Cryolite-
Alumina Melts: Part I. Solubility of FeO, FeAl2O4, NiO,
and NiAl2O4

       TROND E. JENTOFTSEN, ODD-ARNE LORENTSEN, ERNEST W. DEWING,
        GEIR M. HAARBERG, and JOMAR THONSTAD

         The solubilities of FeO, FeAl2O4, NiO, and NiAl2O4 were measured in cryolite-alumina melts at 1020
            8C. FeO was found to be the stable solid phase at alumina concentrations below 5.0 wt pct, while
          FeAl2O4 was stable above that. The corresponding figure for the nickel system was 3.0 wt pct Al2O3.
          These values correspond to Gibbs energies of formation of the aluminates (from the constituent
           oxides) of 217.6 and 229 kJ/mol, respectively. In alumina-saturated melts in the range 980 to 1050
            8C, the solubilities of both aluminates increased with increasing temperature, the apparent enthalpies
            of solution being 65 kJ/mol for FeAl2O4, and 249 kJ/mol for NiAl2O4. Investigation of the solubilities
            of the aluminates as a function of the NaF/AlF3 ratio in alumina-saturated melts at 1020 8C showed
         maxima at a molar ratio of around 5. The results are discussed in terms of the species apparently
            existing in the solution, and are consistent with the solute species being fluorides, not oxyfluorides.
         The activity coefficients of FeF2 (liquid) and NiF2 (solid) in dilute solution in cryolite are found to
          be 0.22 and 1.2, respectively.

  Ferrous oxide (wustite) was prepared in the laboratory by
mixing and dry grinding ferric oxide, Fe2O3 (Aldrich, 99
pct1), with excess iron powder (Aldrich, 99 pct1) in a ball
mill for 28 hours. The mixture was then heated in an iron
crucible under argon atmosphere at 1050 8C to 1100 8C
for 48 hours. Wustite is nonstoichiometric of approximate
composition Fe0.947O. It is thermodynamically stable at tem-
peratures above 571  8C[17] and, below this temperature,
should disproportionate into magnetite, Fe3O4, and iron
(eutectoid  reaction),  but  the  rate  of  disproportionation
decreases rapidly with decreasing temperature. At room tem-
perature, wustite is stable for an unlimited period of time.
To avoid disproportionation, the mixture was quenched by
quickly moving the crucible to a colder part of the furnace
when the temperature had dropped to around 580 8C.
  High-purity green nickel oxide, NiO (Novamet, 99.98 pct,
5 mm), was pressed into tablets and sintered under argon
atmosphere at 1300 8C for 2 hours. Tablets were used to
avoid dispersion of fine NiO particles in the melt, which
could have affected the solubility measurements. That was
not a concern with FeO, because the material was very
coarse.
