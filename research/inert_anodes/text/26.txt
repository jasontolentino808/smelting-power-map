Solutions of Iron Oxides in Molten Cryolite

        ERNEST W. DEWING and JOMAR THONSTAD

           All the iron oxides (FeO, Fe3O4, Fe2O3, and FeAl2O4) dissolve in cryolite-alumina melts to give
            solutions containing both Fe(II) and Fe(III). The factor controlling the Fe(II)/Fe(III) ratio is the oxygen
            pressure, and experimental results are interpreted on that basis. Predictions are made of the variation
            of solubility with oxygen pressure, and the standard potential of the Fe21/Fe31 redox couple is
            calculated. The anode and anode gas of an industrial Hall–Heroult cell appear to be insufficiently
           oxidizing to cause significant conversion of Fe(II) to Fe(III). An anomaly in the liquidus diagrams
            for FeF2 - Na3AlF6 and FeO - Na3AlF6 is accounted for in terms of solid solution of FeF2 in cryolite.

                                                                                                III.  DISCUSSION
                                                 One of the incompletely resolved questions in cryolite
                                                            chemistry is that of identifying the ionic species present in
                                                                 the melts. That there are recognizable species is beyond
                                                       question—both Raman spectra[9] and thermodynamic mod-
                                                                                eling[10,11] have identified species such as (AlF4)2, (AlF5)22,
                                                       and (Al2OFx)(x24)2. The question therefore naturally arises:
                                            When a solute atom, such as Fe, is introduced into the melt,
                                                           with which anions is it closely associated? The question has
                                                                     particular relevance when the source of the solute metal is
                                                        an oxide, and becomes: Is the dissolved metal cation associ-
                                                               ated with fluoride ions only, or with at least one oxide ion?
                                                                    Alternatively, and rather loosely, put: Is the solute metal to
                                                        be regarded as being present as a fluoride or an oxide?
                                                                  In the treatment given so far, no assumptions have been
                                                 made about the answer to that question, but there are features
