Optimisation of power modulation for Hall-H´eroult cells: Process
operability and constraints as virtual energy storage
Choon-Jie Wong a, Adam A. Larkin a, Jie Bao a,*, Maria Skyllas-Kazacos a, Barry J. Welch a,
Nadia Ahli b, Maitha Faraj b, Mohamed Mahmoud b

A R T I C L E  I N F O                  A B S T R A C T

Keywords:                                  Aluminium is manufactured through the Hall-H´eroult process, which is very energy intensive. Power modula­
Virtual energy storage                                   tion, as an industrial-scale demand-side power management approach, allows aluminium smelters to operate
Demand-side power management                   with variable power consumption rates and, as such, be powered by renewable energy sources. In this way,
Hall-h´eroult process                                           aluminium smelting cells can be used as a large virtual energy storage system to balance power demand andOptimisation                                                  supply, and stabilise electrical grids. This paper studies the potential optimal power modulation operatingPower modulation
                                                    conditions, including time-varying line current and anode-cathode distance (ACD) profiles, to maximise the
                                                        profitability of the aluminium reduction cell, subject to constraints on the cell thermal balance. To deal with the
                                           complex cell dynamics which are spatially distributed and multi-timescale, a novel optimisation approach that
                                                           utilises both reduced-order and detailed models is developed. The results yield insights into the optimal line
                                                  current and ACD profiles for different power modulation scenarios, including the time of use electricity tariff and
                                                  spot price. These results can form the foundation for further studies into online control policies of aluminium
                                                 reduction cells.
